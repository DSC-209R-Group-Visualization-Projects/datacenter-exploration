What the PJM capacity auction adds to a monthly bill

PJM, the grid operator for 13 states and DC, buys power plant capacity a few years ahead in an auction. The price jumped about ninefold in the 2025/26 auction. Set your monthly usage to see roughly what that adds to a bill.

Estimate: total auction cost for each delivery year divided by PJM annual load (EIA-930), times your monthly kWh. This spreads cost evenly over every kWh in PJM, so it is a rough average, not a tariff. Households likely carry a larger share than factories, and utilities pass capacity costs through with lags, so real bills differ. The earliest complete year of PJM load in the data is 2019, so 2017/18 and 2018/19 use 2019 load; 2026/27 and 2027/28 use 2025 load. The top chart shows the PJM-wide price; in 2025/26 the Baltimore (BGE) and Dominion zones cleared higher, at $466.35 and $444.26 per MW-day. The price cap and floor (about $325 and $175 per MW-day) were approved by FERC on April 28, 2025. Sources: PJM Base Residual Auction reports (2027/28 report, Table 2), S&P Global, EIA Forms 861 and 930.
