Data Center Strain

The American data center boom

Data Center
Strain
in America

Data centers are being built faster than ever, and a growing number of communities are pushing back. We test whether their worries about the grid, electric bills and water hold up in the data.

Joel Polizzi · Priyanka Nidadavolu · Sanjana Garimella•DSC 209R, UC San Diego

Backstory

Data centers' share of US electricity rose from 1.5% in 2006 to 4.4% in 2023, and it is projected to reach 6.7–12% by 2028.

Backstory

The largest AI campuses are approaching a gigawatt: about 1.7 times the electricity used by every home in Memphis combined.

Chapter 1 · Build-out

The United States currently has 606 operating data centers across 39 states.

Another 975 facilities are planned. If they are all built, the number of tracked sites would grow by 161%.

Chapter 2 · Pushback

1,036 moratoriums in 47 states, and the wave is recent: 170 adopted in June 2026 alone.

Setting aside "time to write rules," 72% name a specific harm. Two lead: the grid and energy, and water.

What we set out to test

Communities say they are worried about the grid and their electric bills, and about water. Are those worries backed by the data?

Chapter 3 · The grid

Today, 65% of operating data centers sit in grid regions rated Elevated risk of power shortfalls.

By 2030, MISO, PJM, ERCOT and parts of the West move to High risk, holding 69% of operating and planned sites. Verdict: Real

Chapter 4 · Electric bills

State by state, more data centers did not mean faster price rises. But states on the PJM grid rose a median +9%, against −1% everywhere else.

Chapter 4 · Electric bills

PJM's capacity auction, paid by every household on the grid, jumped 9.3x, from $28.92 to $269.92 per MW-day.

Removing data centers takes the cost from $14.7 billion down to $5.4 billion: about $9 a month for a typical home. Verdict: Real, but regional

Chapter 5 · Water

About a third of data centers, roughly 500 sites, sit in river basins under High or Extremely high water stress.

But about 36% of Americans live there too, and most sites don't report their water use. Verdict: Plausible, but unmeasured

The scorecard

Two worries hold up. One is still unmeasured.

  • The grid

    Real

    The grid's own regulator names data centers as a main reason the risk of shortfalls is rising.

  • Electric bills

    Real, but regional

    On the PJM grid, data center demand drove most of a capacity cost that adds about $9 a month for a typical home.

  • Water

    Plausible, but unmeasured

    About a third of data centers sit in stressed river basins. How much water they use is mostly unreported.
